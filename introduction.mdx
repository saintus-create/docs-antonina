The Encyclopedia of Rhetoric and Composition is a one-volume scholarly reference covering 2,500 years of rhetorical theory and practice. Across 274 entries — ranging from Aristotle and Cicero to digital discourse and feminist rhetoric — it maps the full breadth of rhetoric as a discipline: its history, its key figures, its foundational terms, and its living applications in law, literature, philosophy, politics, and everyday communication. Whether you are a student encountering rhetoric for the first time or a researcher tracing a specific tradition, this encyclopedia is designed to meet you where you are.

## What You Will Find Here

The encyclopedia covers rhetoric in its widest sense — the oldest and broadest of the humanities, drawing on anthropology, linguistics, philosophy, and psychology. Its entries are organized to serve readers at every level of familiarity with the field.

- **Identifications** — concise definitions of terms, figures, and concepts for quick orientation
- **Notes** — elaborated explanations that situate key topics in their rhetorical context
- **Essays** — in-depth explorations of major subjects, tracing historical development and scholarly debate
- **Articles** — full treatments that illuminate rhetoric's art and methodology with bibliography and cross-references

## Key Features of the Digital Edition

Beyond the editorial content, the digital edition provides several tools to help you navigate and connect ideas across the full corpus of entries.

- **Full-text search** (⌘K / Ctrl\+K) — instant results across all 274 articles
- **AI chat assistant** powered by Claude 3.5 Sonnet — ask questions in plain language and get cited answers drawn from the encyclopedia
- **Interactive knowledge graph** — visualize relationships between entries and discover unexpected connections
- **Alphabetical browsing** — navigate all 23 letter sections from the sidebar or the browse overview
- **LLM-friendly API endpoints** — machine-readable access to encyclopedia content for developers and researchers

<Note>
  This encyclopedia originates from the _Garland Reference Library of the Humanities_, Vol. 1389, edited by **Theresa Enos** and published by Routledge in 1996. The digital edition preserves the original scholarly content while adding modern navigation and AI-assisted discovery tools.
</Note>
