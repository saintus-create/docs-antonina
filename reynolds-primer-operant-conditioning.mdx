# A Primer of Operant Conditioning

<Note>
  **George Stanley Reynolds**’s *A Primer of Operant Conditioning* is a concise introductory text that distills the fundamental principles of operant (instrumental) conditioning for students and general readers. It remains a useful companion to the encyclopedia’s operant-conditioning entries.
</Note>

## What the Primer Covers

The text walks readers through the building blocks of operant conditioning in a structured, example-driven way.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Reinforcement and Punishment" icon="repeat">
    Reynolds explains how consequences shape future behavior, distinguishing **positive** and **negative** reinforcement from **positive** and **negative** punishment. He emphasizes that reinforcement always increases the likelihood of a response, whereas punishment decreases it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The Three-Term Contingency" icon="git-branch">
    The primer frames operant episodes as **Sᴰ → R → Sᴿ** chains: a discriminative stimulus sets the occasion for a response, which is then followed by a reinforcing (or punishing) stimulus. Reynolds uses everyday examples to make this sequence concrete.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Schedules of Reinforcement" icon="calendar">
    A central chapter is devoted to how **intermittent schedules**—fixed-ratio, variable-ratio, fixed-interval, and variable-interval—produce characteristic patterns of responding. Reynolds connects these schedules to real-world behavior, from gambling to workplace productivity.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Extinction and Stimulus Control" icon="x-circle">
    The text covers what happens when reinforcement stops (**extinction**) and how prior reinforcement histories create **stimulus control**, making behavior more likely in some contexts than others.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Why It Matters

<Tip>
  For readers new to behavior analysis, Reynolds’s primer functions as a bridge between everyday intuition about rewards and consequences and the more formal terminology used in the encyclopedia entries on [Basic Concepts (Operant Conditioning)](/docs/basic-concepts-operant) and [Operant Conditioning](/docs/operant-conditioning).
</Tip>
